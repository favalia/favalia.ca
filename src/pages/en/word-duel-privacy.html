Word Duel (“the game”) is made by Favalia (“we”, “us”). This policy explains what the game keeps, why, and the choices you have, on iPhone, iPad and Android alike.

The short version

Word Duel keeps what it needs to run a word game: your player name, your progress, and the words you have played. There is no name, address or date of birth to give, and your contacts are never uploaded. Beyond that, two things leave the game only if you say yes: the advertising identifier the ads use, and a notification token if you turn notifications on. And so the game can be fixed and improved, it counts what happens in it (a word finished, a hint used, a reward taken) and reports crashes, without your name or anything you type.

1. WHAT IS KEPT

  • Your player: the name you choose, a password (stored only as a one-way hash), your avatar and equipped looks, XP, level and coins. Signing in with Apple, Google or Facebook stores the identifier they give us and, where they provide it, your email address, used to recognise you on your next visit and nothing else.
  • Your play: each word and the language it was in, the letters you picked and in what order, whether you solved it, how long it took and what it scored. This is what the Stats tab shows you.
  • Duels and friends: who you played, the words you both faced and the scores, so a duel can be finished and ranked. If you allow notifications, the token that lets a friend's challenge reach your phone.
  • Purchases: Apple (on iPhone and iPad) or Google Play (on Android) tells us a purchase happened and gives us its transaction identifier or purchase token, which we record so the coins are added exactly once. We never see your card, and the store doesn't tell us who you are.
  • Playing as a guest: everything stays on your own device until you make a player, and only then does it move to your account. The language you choose for the app is kept on your device too.

2. ADS

The app shows an ad after every fourth word, and offers one you can choose to watch for extra hearts. They come from Google AdMob, which may use your device's advertising identifier to choose them. On iPhone and iPad, iOS asks your permission first: say no and you still get ads, just less relevant ones. On Android you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your device's settings. In the EEA, the UK and Switzerland, the app asks for your consent before personalised ads on every device. Going ad-free removes the ads between words. The ad for extra hearts is still offered, so the app keeps one ready from AdMob, but it only plays if you choose to watch it. AdMob's own policy is at policies.google.com.

3. NOTIFICATIONS

Only if you allow notifications: a daily reminder to play, which your phone schedules itself; now and then news about the game; and, signed in, a note when a friend challenges you to a duel or asks to be friends. These come through Google's Firebase Cloud Messaging, which gives your device a notification token. Signed in, the server keeps that token with your player, so a friend's challenge reaches your phone, and uses it for nothing else. Signing out, turning the switch off in Settings, or deleting your account removes it.

4. HOW THE GAME IS PLAYED, AND CRASHES

The app uses Google's Firebase Analytics to count what happens in the game: words started and finished, hints and rewards taken, streaks, missions and purchases, with plain details such as the level and language of a word. It uses Firebase Crashlytics to report crashes, with the state of the app at the time. Neither includes your player name, the words you type or your contacts, and the app doesn't let them use your advertising identifier. Signed in, crash reports carry your player's internal number, so a crash can be matched to a problem you write to us about. We use this only to fix the game and decide what to improve. Google's policy for Firebase is at firebase.google.com.

5. CONTACTS

If you invite someone from the app, it asks to read your contacts so it can show you a list to pick from. That list is read on your phone and shown on your phone. It is not uploaded, not stored and not shared, and the invite is an ordinary message that you send yourself.

6. DELETING EVERYTHING

In the app, signed in: Profile › Delete my account. It removes your player, your words and stats, your duels, your friendships and your purchase records. It cannot be undone, and coins you bought are gone with it. If you would rather ask a person, write to {{site:email}}.

7. WHO ELSE SEES IT

Nobody buys it or is sold it. Other players see only what the game shows: your player name, avatar, level, rating, the scores of duels you played with them, your score on the daily word's board for the day, and your points in your weekly league. Beyond that, the only companies involved are the ones that make the game work: the servers it runs on, Apple and Google Play for purchases, AdMob for ads, and Firebase for notifications, analytics and crash reports.
